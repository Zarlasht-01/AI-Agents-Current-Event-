# Project Reflection

## Part A — AI Grading Reflection

### A1. AI Model 1 — Summary

AI Model 1 was ChatGPT. It gave our project a grade of **29/100**.

ChatGPT gave us **18/25** for Content & Presentation, **8/25** for GitHub Collaboration, **3/25** for AI Evaluation Setup, and **0/25** for Reflection Quality.

ChatGPT said that our topic was relevant and appropriate for a basic computer science course. It also said that our project connected AI agents to important issues such as work, privacy, security, human oversight, and unintended actions.

The main problems identified by ChatGPT were GitHub collaboration, the incomplete AI evaluation section, and the unfinished reflection.

ChatGPT also said that some parts of the video could not be verified.

### A2. AI Model 2 — Summary

AI Model 2 was Google Gemini. It gave our project a grade of **20/100**.

Gemini gave us **20/25** for Content & Presentation and **0/25** for GitHub Collaboration, **0/25** for AI Evaluation Setup, and **0/25** for Reflection Quality.

Gemini said that the video clearly explained AI agents and the difference between traditional chatbots and AI agents. It also said that the discussion question was strong and that the presentation discussed societal and ethical risks.

Gemini identified two problems with the presentation: the video was **3 minutes and 1 second**, which is one second longer than the required maximum, and only one student appeared to present the video.

Gemini could not verify the GitHub collaboration, AI evaluation files, or reflection.

### A3. Comparison — What Was Similar? What Was Different?

The two AI models agreed that our topic was relevant and that the presentation explained AI agents clearly.

Both AI models also liked our discussion question about how much control people should give AI agents when making decisions and taking actions.

Both models identified problems with the parts of the project that could not be verified. They both gave us very low scores for the GitHub collaboration, AI evaluation setup, and reflection.

The main difference was the final grade. ChatGPT gave us **29/100**, while Gemini gave us **20/100**.

Another difference was that ChatGPT gave us some points for GitHub Collaboration and AI Evaluation Setup, while Gemini gave us zero points for those categories because it could not access or verify the repository.

We think ChatGPT's evaluation was more useful for our project because it was able to review more of our GitHub materials.

### A4. Do We Agree With the AI's Grade?

We partly agree with the AI grades.

We agree that our reflection was incomplete at the time of the evaluations. We also understand why the AI models gave low scores for the reflection and AI evaluation sections.

We also agree that GitHub collaboration needed stronger evidence. We have now worked on the repository by adding the complete AI Model 1 and AI Model 2 evaluations.

However, we think some of the low scores were caused by the AI models being unable to verify information from the repository. Our GitHub repository was available to us, and we were able to work on it and make commits.

We also noticed that Gemini identified the video as being 3 minutes and 1 second. This is an important issue because the assignment limit was 3 minutes.

### A5. What Would We Do Differently?

If we could do the project again, we would start the GitHub collaboration and AI evaluation requirements earlier.

We would make sure that both group members have clear GitHub contributions from the beginning.

We would also create and complete the AI evaluation files before the final deadline instead of leaving them unfinished.

We would complete the reflection immediately after receiving the two AI evaluations.

We would also check the final video length carefully before submitting it so that it stays within the required time limit.

## Part B — AI Research Assistance Reflection

### B1. How We Used AI During Research

We used AI as a research and learning tool during the project.

AI helped us understand the topic of AI agents and explain the difference between traditional chatbots and AI agents in simpler language.

We also used AI to help organize ideas, understand information, and evaluate our completed project according to the grading rubric.

### B2. What Worked Well

One thing that worked well was using AI to explain technical ideas in simple language.

For example, AI helped us understand the idea that AI agents can work toward a goal, complete multiple steps, and use tools instead of only responding to a single question.

AI was also useful for reviewing our project and showing us areas that needed improvement.

### B3. What Did Not Work Well

One problem we experienced was that AI could not always verify information from our GitHub repository or video.

For example, Gemini gave us zero points for several categories because it said the GitHub repository was inaccessible. This showed us that an AI evaluation can depend on whether the AI is able to access the information it needs.

We also learned that AI should not automatically be trusted. Important information should be checked against the original sources and the actual project materials.

### B4. Overall Reflection

Overall, AI was useful during our project because it helped us understand the topic, organize our ideas, and identify weaknesses in our work.

The two AI evaluations also showed us that different AI models can give different grades even when they are given similar instructions.

We learned that AI can be a helpful tool, but it does not replace human judgment or checking information.

If we completed another project like this, we would use AI again, but we would verify important information ourselves and make sure all assignment requirements were completed before submitting the project.
